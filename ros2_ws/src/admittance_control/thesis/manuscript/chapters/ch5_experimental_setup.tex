is measured, and how one experiment is run; the last section describes the setup of the WeldSet
benchmark. Chapter~\ref{chp:results} reports the results.

\section{Robot, Camera and Tool}
\label{sec:setup_robot}
Table~\ref{tab:hardware} lists the hardware. The UR5e carries a six-axis force/torque sensor at
its flange, read at 500~Hz; the cell uses only the force, never the torque, for touch detection,
collision aborts, the table touches and the check of the tool-centre point. The camera is an Intel
RealSense of the D435 series, mounted on the wrist beside the pen; the cell's own camera node aligns
depth to colour and streams both at $1280\times720$ and 30~Hz (under the driver's name for the
unaligned stream, which can mislead a reader of the topic list). Its stereo baseline is 50~mm and its
minimum depth at this resolution about 0.28~m, the visibility floor that the view planner enforces
(the close views themselves are at 0.40~m).

\paragraph{The pen holder and camera mount.} The tool was designed for this work and 3D printed
(Figure~\ref{fig:tool}). It is one assembly bolted to the flange: a flange plate with a side arm
that carries the camera, on the side opposite the robot's tool connector, a holder body for the pen,
and two clamp pieces (named ``chuck'' in the CAD model) just below the holder body, 44--59~mm above
the flange face. The assembly measures $94\times126\times140$~mm; the holder
body reaches from 60 to 140~mm above the flange face. The pen tip, with the cap off, sits at
$(1.07,\,-1.35,\,180.91)$~mm in the flange frame (the tool-centre calibration of
\S\ref{sec:cal_tcp}). The camera's optical axis is parallel to the flange's $z$ axis, which the tool
model takes as the pen axis, within about $0.2^\circ$, with its optical centre at about $(34.8,\,89.0,\,54.5)$~mm in the flange frame (the calibrated
extrinsic, Chapter~\ref{chp:results}). The collision envelope of the planner
(\S\ref{sec:mc_collision}) was derived from the assembly's CAD model, with a 3~mm radial margin on the holder's capsules, and
the camera body was measured on the bench; the boxes of the flange adapter and the camera arm have no
documented source. The camera's cable runs round the wrist, which is why
the last wrist joint is kept within $\pm180^\circ$ of its home value (\S\ref{sec:mc_reach}).

\section{Test Parts}
\label{sec:setup_parts}
The test parts cover the seam families of the cell (Table~\ref{tab:setup_parts},
Figure~\ref{fig:setup_parts}). The ear and the first base plate are cut from 8~mm MDF stock; the current
base plate is 5.5~mm thick. The tubes, the rounded-rectangle section and the band were 3D printed on a
Creality K1 Max, each from one watertight CAD model in millimetres, from which both the printed STL
and the cell's PLY are exported. The print list prescribes a matte, light filament: black, glossy or
translucent filament gives the camera holes or wrong depth. Walls are at least 3~mm, tubes are printed upright and bands and plates lying flat, and the print
list prescribes measuring every part with callipers after printing. Every part is in the CAD registry, fitted to
its exact primitive (\S\ref{sec:mc_registry}).

\section{Measuring the Mark Error}
\label{sec:setup_measure}
The reference for every accuracy claim must not depend on the camera. Two measurements satisfy
this. The first is the pen's own contact (Equation~\ref{eq:touch}): every descent stops at the first
1.5~N touch, so the tip's position at that moment, from the robot's calibrated kinematics, is a point
on the real part, and its signed distance from the registered point along the pen axis, the contact
depth $\delta_c$, is the registration's error in that direction. Because a T-joint's two fillets are
touched along axes each $45^\circ$ off the vertical, on opposite sides of the standing plate, early contact on both sides indicates a vertical error and early
contact on one side with late contact on the other a sideways shift; 1~mm of height error reads as
about 1.4~mm of contact depth along a $45^\circ$ pen. The second is the mark itself, a pen dot on the
part, whose distance from the true weld root is measured on the part.

Two further tools are used for evaluation only, never fed back into the cell. A probe reports, for a
lightly free-driven pen tip, its height above the touched table and its signed distance to every
registered face, which separates the causes of an early contact. An offline tool fits, to the
recorded contacts of a tube on a plate, the real tube's lateral offset and the real plate's height by
grid search, and reports each mark's distance from the real root; for a band on a plate it reports the
contacts against the registered surfaces without a fit.

\section{Experimental Procedure}
\label{sec:setup_procedure}
One run proceeds as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Start.} The robot at its scan home, the External Control program running, the marking
  node in dry run until enabled (the multi-view refinement and the aim move as soon as they are
  called). The calibration chain is checked, not redone: the table height
  under the pen and the camera's view of the table (\S\ref{sec:mc_calibration}).
\item \textbf{Per part.} Place the part; aim the camera by pointing; capture; click the part;
  register; move the part into its place while it is tracked; hold it still for a few seconds; save.
\item \textbf{Refine.} Run the multi-view refinement; record which parts were accepted and the
  camera estimate.
\item \textbf{Compute.} Compute the seams and tacks; run the reachability check.
\item \textbf{Mark.} Plan; execute tack by tack, dots for accuracy; the marking record holds every
  contact.
\item \textbf{Measure.} Measure the marks on the part; archive the run with its assembly.
\end{enumerate}

\section{WeldSet Benchmark Setup}
\label{sec:setup_weldset}
The benchmark corpus has twelve strata of 60 scenes (\S\ref{sec:ws_corpus}), generated on the
desktop, which holds the authoritative copy of every corpus, the rendered set and the batch results;
the laptop's copies are stale. The batch runs the seven literature methods over every condition of
\S\ref{sec:ws_protocol} in resumable chunks. The two proposed extractors run from their own scripts on
the same 720 scenes, on clean clouds only, in both views, with the same 3~mm tolerance, and without
the fixture twins and the L1 level.
