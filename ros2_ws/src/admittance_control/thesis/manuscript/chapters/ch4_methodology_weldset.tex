Seam-extraction methods are evaluated against hand-annotated labels on data that is
rarely released, and the annotation itself is less accurate than the accuracy claimed.
WeldSet removes the annotation: the scene is built from the seam outward, so the seam is
known exactly. This chapter describes how scenes are constructed
(\S\ref{sec:ws_goals}--\S\ref{sec:ws_sensor}), the corpus (\S\ref{sec:ws_corpus}), how
extractors are evaluated (\S\ref{sec:ws_protocol}), the two seam extractors proposed in
this work (\S\ref{sec:ws_own}), and how WeldSet and the robotic cell of
Chapter~\ref{chp:method_cell} share one geometry (\S\ref{sec:ws_link}).

% -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Design Goals}
\label{sec:ws_goals}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ground truth is constructed, never detected.} The generator samples a
  joint, places the parts by closed-form transforms and computes the seam from the same
  transforms, exact to floating point. No annotator and no estimator are in the label
  path.
\item \textbf{Every condition is a dial.} Thickness, gap, angle, noise, view and
  fixture are parameters; one seed gives identical twin scenes across any one dial.
\item \textbf{Every choice is a versioned rule}, and every corpus passes gates that would
  catch a shortcut or an approximation.
\item \textbf{Accessible:} the first tier needs no renderer and no GPU.
\end{itemize}

\section{Curved Seams by Construction}
\label{sec:ws_curves}
For curved joints the order is reversed once more: the seam curve is drawn first and the
parts are derived from it, so the seam is exact by construction. Seven families cover
the cases of the literature: a line, a circle (a tube on a plate), an ellipse (a tilted
tube), a saddle (a branch on a pipe), a rounded rectangle (a box section), a swept path (a
curved strip) and an arc butt.

\section{Seams by the Accessibility Rule}
\label{sec:ws_d4}
A seam is not every place where two parts touch: it is where they touch \emph{and} a
torch can reach. The rule (D4) takes every pair of exterior faces of two parts that meet,
computes their nominal seam as the intersection of the two face planes
(Eq.~\ref{eq:plane_intersection}), and keeps it when the bisector of the dihedral angle
escapes to free space. Each seam gets a class (fillet, butt, lap toe, edge); a rejected
candidate is kept with the reason.

\section{Tack Placement Rule}
\label{sec:ws_tacks}
Tacks are a rule applied to the seams, versioned and re-runnable, not a label. With $t$
the thinner member and $L$ the length of an open seam, tackrule-0.1 sets
\begin{gather}
\ell_{\mathrm{tack}} = \operatorname{clip}(4t,\,10,\,50)\ \mathrm{mm},\qquad
d_{\max} = \min(33t,\,400)\ \mathrm{mm},\qquad d_{\min} = 10t,
\label{eq:tackrule_len}\\
m = \max(2t,\ \ell_{\mathrm{tack}}),\qquad
n = \max\!\left(2,\ \ceil*{\frac{L-2m}{d_{\max}}}+1\right),
\label{eq:tackrule_n}
\end{gather}
with the tacks evenly spaced between the two end margins $m$. A closed seam takes an even
number $n\ge4$ with a deterministic phase. The rule also stores a weld order: the ends,
then the centre, then by bisection (opposite pairs on a loop), with seams of the same class
interleaved.

\section{Evaluation Protocol for Seam Extractors}
\label{sec:ws_protocol}
Seven published methods were re-implemented, each with its own notebook recording every
reading of the paper: plane RANSAC with pairwise intersection, normal-based region
growing, local-bounding-box flatness with a curve fit, point-pair-feature voting for
plane pairs, PCA slicing, CAD registration with the seam transferred from the model, and
quadric surface fits \cite{yi2026seam,wei_regiongrow,zhang_lobb,wang2024ppfseam,wang2026pipes,fang2024modelreg,li2026quadric}.
\TODO{Check every citation key against the methods (MANUAL \S8.1 table).}

\textbf{The oracle ladder.} Most of these pipelines start with a learned coarse stage
that was not released. At level L0 each method receives \emph{its own paper's} coarse
stage from the ground truth, so that no method is handicapped by a stage its authors did
not publish; L1 withholds it and changes nothing else.

\textbf{Metrics.} F1 at a 3~mm tolerance over densified polylines; the symmetric Chamfer
distance; the lateral error distribution (its 95th percentile is what a tool path
cares about); the literature's own matched-path RMSE; the earth mover's distance as a
coverage check.

\textbf{Conditions.} Two views (a full exterior scan and a single camera), noise levels,
the fixture on and off, repeated seeds for the randomised methods.

\subsection{Footprint Extraction from Sequential Scans}
\label{sec:ws_footprint}
\textbf{Idea.} Scan the base part $A$ alone, then the assembly $A+B$. Whatever the first
scan does not explain belongs to $B$, so the segmentation of the assembly comes from the
order of placement instead of from a learned model. Where $B$ rests on $A$, each part
leaves a footprint on the other.

\textbf{Method.} The base scan is registered into the assembly scan; points of the
assembly that are far from the base scan are $B$. For each part, a bounded distance field
to the other part is computed, and the footprints are the points closer than the fit-up
gap $g$:
\begin{equation}
F_A = \{\vect{p}\in A : D_B(\vect{p}) < g\},\qquad F_B = \{\vect{p}\in B : D_A(\vect{p}) < g\}.
\label{eq:footprint}
\end{equation}
Each footprint point slides along the negative gradient of the distance field until the
distance reaches $g$. An exposure filter keeps the points where both parts are visible
nearby; the result is clustered into seams, ordered (the longest path of a minimum
spanning tree), smoothed by a spline and resampled. A joint frame follows at each point
from the two leg directions, and a ball rolled in each cross-section gives the toes, the
leg lengths and the bead area.

\subsection{Matching and Sphere-Tracing on Labelled Clouds}
\label{sec:ws_seamfind}
\textbf{Idea.} Given two point clouds that are already labelled by part, points of $A$ and
$B$ that are each other's nearest neighbour lie near the seam; from there, each side can
walk towards the other over its own surface until the two meet.

\textbf{Method.} Normals are estimated per part, so they do not blur across the joint,
and each part gets a moving-least-squares projection $\Pi_A$, $\Pi_B$. A cheap
accessibility filter removes points that no torch could see (for example the hidden
contact face of a lap joint). Seeds are mutual nearest neighbours between farthest-point
samples of $A$ and $B$ closer than the gap bound. From each seed, a walker on $A$ moves
towards $B$ by sphere tracing:
\begin{equation}
\vect{x}_{k+1} = \Pi_A\!\Big(\vect{x}_k + \alpha\, d_k\,\frac{\vect{t}_k}{\norm{\vect{t}_k}}\Big),
\qquad d_k = \norm{\vect{x}_k-\Pi_B(\vect{x}_k)},
\qquad \vect{t}_k = \vect{u}_k - (\vect{u}_k^{\top}\vect{n}_k)\,\vect{n}_k,
\label{eq:sphere_trace}
\end{equation}
where $\vect{u}_k$ points from $\vect{x}_k$ to its projection on $B$ and $\vect{n}_k$ is $A$'s
normal. The step is the distance to $B$ projected onto $A$'s tangent plane, so the walker
cannot overshoot, and it stops when it touches $B$ or stalls at a gap. A mirror walker
runs on $B$. The two end points are the toes; their distance is the gap. The joint angle
is estimated twice, from the normals and from the walker's step ratio, and the
disagreement is a quality flag. Root points are clustered into seams, ordered, smoothed
and given a torch frame, and a torch-cone check marks each span weldable or not.

\section{WeldSet and the Robotic Cell: One Geometry}
\label{sec:ws_link}
WeldSet and the cell are connected in two directions.

\textbf{From WeldSet to the cell.} The cell computes seams with WeldSet's own geometry:
each registered CAD part becomes a WeldSet primitive (\S\ref{sec:mc_registry}); the seam
rule (\S\ref{sec:ws_d4}), the curve constructors (\S\ref{sec:ws_curves}) and the tack
rule (\S\ref{sec:ws_tacks}) are applied unchanged. The seams and tacks the robot marks are
therefore WeldSet's exact ground truth, evaluated at the measured poses; their error is
the registration's, which the pen's contact measures independently
(Eq.~\ref{eq:touch}).

\textbf{From the cell to WeldSet.} The two proposed extractors take as input what the
cell's procedure already produces. The footprint method needs a scan of the base before
the next part is placed: the cell registers and saves the parts one at a time, so that
scan exists. The matching method needs clouds labelled by part: the cell segments every
part and registers it, so its points carry a label. Both are CAD-free, so they can check
the CAD-based seams of the cell, or replace them where no CAD exists, and WeldSet scores
them against exact truth before they run on the robot.
